Most happy guests never leave a review. Not because they didn't enjoy the meal, but because nobody asked, and by the time they're home they've forgotten. Unhappy guests, on the other hand, often find the time.

So the reviews you get can say more about who remembered than about how good you are. You can change that.

## Ask while they're still at the table

The best moment to ask is when the meal is fresh: after the food, before they leave. A guest who has just enjoyed a plate is happy to tap a few stars.

A review screen on the table page makes this easy. The guest rates the meal, picks what stood out, like the food, the service or the vibe, and adds a line if they want.

## Send happy guests to Google

When a guest gives four or five stars, offer them a one-tap way to share it on Google. They have already written what they think, so it takes seconds.

This is how a restaurant with great service but few reviews starts to show it.

## Hear unhappy guests first

When a guest gives one to three stars, their note comes to you privately. You get the chance to fix the problem, apologise or reach out, before it becomes a public review.

Often a quick reply turns a bad night into a second visit.

## Make it part of the routine

- Put the review option on every table page.
- Ask staff to mention it when they clear the plates.
- Read the private notes each week and act on what repeats.

More reviews come from asking at the right time. Better reviews come from listening to what they tell you.
