A QR menu is your menu, on the guest's own phone. There is a small code on each table. The guest points their camera at it, and your menu opens in their browser. No app to download and no account to make.

## What happens when a guest scans

The camera reads the code and opens a link. That link is special: it knows which restaurant it belongs to and which table it is on. So the page that opens is your page, in your colours, and every order from it already carries the table number.

From there the guest can:

- read the menu, with photos, prices and dietary tags
- add dishes and send the order to the kitchen
- call a waiter, or ask for the bill
- join Wi-Fi without asking anyone for the password

## Why one code per table matters

A single code by the door shows a menu. A code on every table does more, because the table is part of the link. When an order or a waiter call comes in, your team sees exactly where to go. Nobody shouts across the room, and nobody guesses.

## What changes for your team

Paper menus get printed once and then go out of date. A QR menu changes the moment you do. Run out of tilapia at 8pm? Mark it sold out, and every table sees it straight away. Raise a price? It is right on the next scan.

Your staff spend less time walking menus to tables and taking orders by hand, and more time on the guests in front of them.

## Getting started

You need three things: your menu, a printer and a few minutes.

1. Add your dishes, prices and photos.
2. Print one code for each table.
3. Set the cards out and watch the first orders come in.

That's it. There is no hardware to buy and nothing for your guests to install.
